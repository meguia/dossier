IRIS

Rotating discs, changing sound

Leonardo Salzano / Manuel Eguía · LAPSo

A kinetic acoustic sculpture of two independently rotating, perforated discs. Their relative alignment changes how sound passes through the structure, affecting timbre and spatial projection.

Developed at LAPSo from Leonardo Salzano’s original design, IRIS forms part of our work on acoustic materials for performance. We combine physical experimentation, construction and programming to make the geometry of the sculpture adjustable during a musical work.

Placed between a source and listeners, the discs alter the transmission of particular frequency bands. Rotation changes the relationship between the two layers, allowing the sculpture to modify the sound’s spectral balance and apparent location.

An acoustic material for performance

IRIS and Sonic Crystal Room belong to the same line of research into how periodic structures can modify sound in air. IRIS concentrates this investigation in a moving object whose alignment becomes a parameter for performance.

Acoustic measurements and a study by Leonardo Salzano and Manuel Eguía examine how the rotating layers change sound focusing, timbre and spatial perception. The 2022 paper connects the sculpture’s material design with musical applications.
