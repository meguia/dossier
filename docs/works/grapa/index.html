Proyecto GRAPa

Listening to a voice and the place that answers it

Proyecto GRAPa · Manuel Eguía, Francisco Durante, Damián Payo, Mauro Zannoli

An enquiry into acoustic heritage through the relationship between copla singing, its communities and the natural spaces in which it is practised.

GRAPa — Grupo de Relevamiento Acústico del Patrimonio — brings together acoustic measurement, immersive audiovisual recording and documentary filmmaking. I founded the project to investigate and document the relationship between musical practices and their acoustic environments.

La Copla y el Anfiteatro de la Quebrada de las Conchas centres on a natural amphitheatre in the Calchaquí Valleys. With the active participation of coplera Mariana Carrizo and local singers, the project records the interplay of voices, cajas and rock walls. The acoustic character of the place is part of the musical experience.

Three ways of documenting a listening culture

Immersive copla recordings place the listener within the singing circle, preserving the spatial relationships between performers. The work is grounded in the knowledge and participation of the singers, including Mariana Carrizo.

Ambisonic and binaural impulse-response measurements capture how the amphitheatre responds to sound. These recordings allow aspects of its acoustic character to be recreated through loudspeakers or headphones.

A documentary follows the April 2018 fieldwork, bringing together the acoustic survey and the recording of coplas. Together, the three strands present music and the space of its creation as a shared acoustic heritage.
