The Wind Chimes

Tubular instruments for a bridge and a river

Buenos Aires Sonora / LAPSo

Five large tubular wind chimes, suspended from the Nicolás Avellaneda transporter bridge, were played during a collective sound performance over the Riachuelo.

With Buenos Aires Sonora and the LAPSo team, we built instruments for El Puente Suena II [Invocaciones], presented within Sigismond de Vajay’s Lluvia de Arañas sobre el Riachuelo.

We designed the instruments around resonating metal tubes and a suspended striking mechanism. Performers set them in motion from below, making the bridge, the river and the space between them part of the performance.

Medium

From the laboratory to the river

We built and tested the tubular instruments at LAPSo before installing them on the bridge. The installation photographs show the instruments being assembled, suspended and activated from a boat below.

The wind chimes formed one part of Buenos Aires Sonora’s performance. Other performers used the bridge’s metal structure as a sound source, alongside recorded voices and live electronics.

Instrument concept: Hernán Kerlleñevich. Design: Manuel Eguía. Construction: Manuel Eguía, Ignacio Spiousas, Mauricio Biviano, Pablo Riera and Pablo Etchemendy. Performers on the large chimes: Manuel Eguía, Ignacio Spiousas, Mauricio Biviano, Pablo Riera and Ramiro Vergara.
